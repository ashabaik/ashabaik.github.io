Dasta: a wholesale marketplace in English and Arabic.

Dasta is a portfolio project: a working wholesale marketplace for shops, cafes and offices, designed and built end to end. Verified suppliers, price breaks by quantity, payment protected until delivery, and messages that stay on the platform. Suppliers, products, prices and orders are sample data, and payments run in test mode.

What it does

  • Three kinds of account with their own screens: buyer, supplier, admin.
  • A catalogue of 40 sample products in six categories, with search, categories and sorting.
  • Wholesale terms on every listing: unit, minimum order quantity, lead time and price breaks. The database enforces the minimum and works out the price; the browser never sends a price.
  • At most 10 listings per supplier, enforced in the database and safe when requests arrive together.
  • Supplier accounts that keep public data (code, region) and company details (name, phone, address, bank) in separate database schemas.
  • Buyer-supplier messages with live updates and a contact-details check: phone numbers in Latin and Arabic digits, spaced or spelled out, e-mails, links, handles. Moderation queue and strikes for the admin.
  • Admin tools: approve suppliers, open company details with a recorded reason, disputes, payouts, commission.
  • English and Arabic with right-to-left layout, designed for the phone first.

Protected payment

The buyer pays Dasta, not the supplier. An order moves through fixed steps: paid and held, shipped, delivery confirmed, paid to the supplier minus commission; or refunded, or disputed. Every movement goes into a double-entry ledger that cannot be edited.

Payments run in test mode through a mock provider that sends signed callbacks in the same shape as Paymob's, and each callback is applied at most once. No card is charged and no real money moves.

Design

  • A white page with warm light surfaces, heavy headings and one deep green; tall photographs with no boxes.
  • A directed home page: one opening sequence, one held scene where the categories travel sideways with the scroll (mirrored in Arabic), and a designed last screen. Working pages stay still and fast.
  • Motion is off for visitors who prefer reduced motion, and the pages work with JavaScript off (the one exception is the "page not found" screen).

How supplier data is kept private

  • Company details live in a schema that buyer and supplier sessions have no database privilege on.
  • Row level security on every table; pages query the database with the visitor's own session.
  • The admin can open company details only through a function that writes the audit log first.
  • Photos are re-encoded on the server, so camera data and GPS position are removed.

Proof

  • 130 database tests, 97 unit tests and a crawl test that opens every page as six kinds of visitor.
  • Private fields in the sample data carry markers; a test fails if any marker reaches a buyer or another supplier through a table, a function, a page or a header.
  • Lighthouse 12 on the live home page, phone profile, 17 November 2026, 15 runs per language: accessibility 100 and best practices 100 in every run; performance 90 to 99; LCP median 2.29 s in English and 2.43 s in Arabic (range 1.88 to 2.67 s).
  • Automated accessibility scan (axe, WCAG 2.1 A and AA) on six page types in both languages: 0 findings. This is an automated scan only: no screen reader and no real phone were used.

What it is not

  • Not a trading business. No real money, no real suppliers or buyers. The data is reset every night.
  • The rules in Egypt for holding payments, tax and invoicing were not checked.
